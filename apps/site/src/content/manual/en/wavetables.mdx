A wavetable is a row of single-cycle waves (frames), usually 2048 samples each and up to 256 in a table. An
oscillator of Chisper plays one frame at a time, and its **Wave** knob moves through them, so the tone changes as you
turn it or as an envelope or LFO turns it for you. Pick a table from the library, import a WAV wavetable you
already have (other synths and wavetable packs use the same format), turn any short audio file into a table, or
draw a wave by hand.

## What is a wavetable?

A single cycle of a wave repeated over and over is a tone: its shape is its timbre. A wavetable lines up many such
cycles, from a sine to a saw, from one vowel to another, from a clean tone to a growl, and the oscillator plays the
one at the position of **Wave**.

- **A frame** is one cycle, 2048 samples in most tables. It plays at the pitch of the note, whatever its length in
  samples.
- **A table** holds up to 256 frames. **Wave** picks one; the strip under the wave shows where you are
  (*Wave 33/256*).
- **A morph table** has few frames but plays as 256: Chisper fills the gaps with crossfades of the frames on either
  side, so a turn of **Wave** sweeps smoothly from one to the next. A table says it is one in the marker of its file
  (below), and every table made from an audio file is one.
- **No clicks:** when **Wave** moves, a voice fades into the new frame over one cycle of its note (5 ms at most).

## The library

The name of the table sits between **◀ ▶** at the top of each oscillator. The arrows step through every table of
the library, folder after folder; a click on the name opens the browser.

Use the arrow keys too: <kbd>↑</kbd> <kbd>↓</kbd> within a column, <kbd>→</kbd> <kbd>←</kbd> between the folders
and the tables. *Init* plays *Basic Shapes* from the Basics folder. Tables download when you pick them; the bar
under the name shows the download.

## The wavetable editor

Click the pencil at the top left of the wave of an oscillator. The editor fills the screen; on its left are two
tabs, **Manual** (the highlighter: draw) and **Audio file** (the speaker: import and make), and the cross at the
top closes it.

What you change plays at once: there is no Apply button and no undo. Picking another table in the oscillator
replaces your work.

Both tabs have the strip of frames on top: click a frame (or turn its **Wave** knob) to edit it; the oscillator
moves to it too.

### How do I draw a wave?

<Figure name='editor-draw' alt='The Manual tab of the wavetable editor: the strip of the frames of the table on top, the frame being edited on a grid, the Wave, Grid X and Grid Y knobs on the left'>

The Manual tab: the frames on top, the frame you edit on its grid.

</Figure>

Press on the chart and drag. Every column of the grid you pass over takes the height under the pointer, snapped to
the grid. A mouse, a finger or a pen all draw.

- **Grid X**: the number of columns, 4 to 256 (16 by default). Few columns give steps and squares; many give
  detail.
- **Grid Y**: how finely the height snaps: 2, 5, 10 or 20 steps from the middle to the top (5 by default).
- **+** at the end of the strip adds a copy of the current frame at the end of the table, up to 256 frames.

<Tip>

Draw a few frames that differ a little, then turn **Wave** with an LFO or an envelope: even a rough drawing moves
like a real wavetable.

</Tip>

### How do I import a WAV wavetable?

1. Open the **Audio file** tab.
2. Drop the file onto it, or press **Choose a file**.
3. The table plays at once, the editor shows its frames on the Manual tab, and a message says how many it has.

A file is taken as a wavetable when:

- it is a WAV with a `clm` marker, the marker of the wavetables of other synths: its frame size and whether it is
  a morph table are read from it; **or**
- it is a WAV of whole 2048-sample frames, 1 to 256 of them (many wavetable packs have no marker).

The file may be 8, 16, 24 or 32-bit PCM or 32 or 64-bit float, mono or stereo (the left channel is used). Its
sample rate doesn't matter: the frames are read as they are, never resampled. Of a table with a marker and more
than 256 frames, the first 256 play, and the message says so.

### How do I make a wavetable from an audio file?

<Figure name='editor-audio' alt='The Audio file tab of the wavetable editor with a bass sound loaded: the frames cut from it on top, the sound with the cut windows marked, the current frame below, the W Count, Size and Phase knobs'>

An audio file cut into 32 frames: the windows are marked on the sound, the frame you picked is below.

</Figure>

Any other audio file is cut into frames: drop it or press **Choose a file** (WAV, MP3, OGG, FLAC, M4A, whatever your
browser plays), up to 12 seconds long. Or try one of the examples: *Square Wave*, *707 Kick*, *Bass sound*.

- **W Count**: how many frames to cut, 1 to 64 (32 by default), evenly spaced over the whole file.
- **Size**: the length of each frame, 16 to 2048 samples (512 by default). The frame becomes one cycle, so a
  shorter Size plays a shorter piece of the sound at the pitch of the note.
- **Phase**: shifts all the cuts later, by up to one step between two of them.
- The folder key under the knobs goes back to the drop screen for another file.

The result is a morph table: it plays as 256 crossfaded frames, so turning **Wave** sweeps through the sound from
its start to its end.

**What makes a good source?** One note held at a steady pitch: a sung vowel, a bass note, a synth pad. Chisper
doesn't look for the pitch, it cuts where the knobs say, so a frame loops cleanly only when it holds whole cycles of
the sound. At 44.1 kHz a frame of 512 samples is exactly one cycle of 86 Hz (a little under F2), one of 1024 is
one cycle of 43 Hz: a note near those pitches, or an octave above them, gives the cleanest frames. A drum hit or a
noisy texture works too, as a rougher, grittier table.

<Note>

The audio file stays loaded while the page is open, and going back to the **Audio file** tab cuts it into the
oscillator again, over anything you drew since. Press the folder key first if you want to keep your drawing.

</Note>

## Where does my own table live?

Only in the oscillator of the page you made it in. A preset saves the name of a library table, not your frames:
after a reload, and for anyone who opens your preset or its link, the library table plays instead.

That is why the logo of the synth opens this site in a new tab: the synth keeps your table in its own.

## Why isn't my file accepted?

- **"… is not an audio file"**: its type and name are neither audio nor `.wav`, `.aiff`, `.flac`, `.mp3` and the
  like. A dropped file that isn't audio isn't taken at all.
- **"… is longer than 12 seconds"**: trim the audio; only a WAV wavetable may be longer.
- **"… can't be read as audio"**: your browser can't decode it (a rare codec, a damaged file). Convert it to WAV.
- A table of the library that doesn't load stops its bar under the name; the oscillator keeps the table it had.
  *Not enough memory for the table* or *The table failed to build* say the same, with **Retry**.
