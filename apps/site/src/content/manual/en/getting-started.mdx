Chisper is a wavetable synthesizer that runs in your browser. Open [chisper.qvantor.org](/), click anywhere and play
the letter keys of your computer keyboard: <kbd>Z</kbd> is C3, <kbd>Q</kbd> is C4. A popular preset is already
loaded; turn any knob to change it, then save the result as a preset with a link anyone can open. No download, no
account.

This page takes you through the first ten minutes: starting the sound, playing it, finding presets, the parts of
the screen, the first knobs to turn and saving a sound of your own.

## Step 1: open Chisper and start the sound

Open [chisper.qvantor.org](/) in Chrome, Edge, Firefox or Safari (16.4 or later), on a computer, a Chromebook or a
phone. Every start opens a random preset from the Popular list, so the first sound is a surprise; its name is in
the header.

Browsers keep a page silent until you touch it, so the sound starts with your first click, tap or key press
anywhere on the page. There is no start button to look for.

<Note>

A MIDI keyboard alone doesn't wake the sound up: the browser waits for the page itself to be clicked, tapped or
typed on. If you play only MIDI, click the page once first.

</Note>

## Step 2: play it

### How do I play a synth with my computer keyboard?

Two rows of letters are two octaves of a piano. The bottom row starts at <kbd>Z</kbd> = C3 and goes up the white
keys <kbd>Z</kbd> <kbd>X</kbd> <kbd>C</kbd> <kbd>V</kbd> <kbd>B</kbd> <kbd>N</kbd> <kbd>M</kbd>, with the black keys
on the row above it (<kbd>S</kbd> <kbd>D</kbd>, <kbd>G</kbd> <kbd>H</kbd> <kbd>J</kbd>). The top row starts an
octave higher at <kbd>Q</kbd> = C4, its black keys on the digits (<kbd>2</kbd> <kbd>3</kbd>, <kbd>5</kbd> <kbd>6</kbd>
and <kbd>7</kbd>), and runs up to <kbd>]</kbd> = G5.

<KeyboardMap />

- <kbd>↓</kbd> and <kbd>↑</kbd> move both rows down or up an octave, from octave 0 to octave 6. The browser
  remembers where you left them. The box left of the on-screen keys shows the octave (C3) and has the same arrows.
- The keys go by their place on the keyboard, not by their letter: on a German keyboard the key
  labelled <kbd>Y</kbd> plays C3 (it sits where <kbd>Z</kbd> is), on a French one <kbd>W</kbd> does.
- Nothing plays while <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> or <kbd>⌘</kbd> is held or while you type in a text field, so shortcuts and
  the preset search make no noise.
- Every note from the computer keyboard has the same velocity (100). For soft and hard notes, use a
  [MIDI keyboard](/manual/midi-keyboard-setup/).

### The on-screen piano and the pitch wheel

The bottom row of the screen is a piano: as many octaves as the window fits (up to five), starting an octave below
the computer keyboard. Click the keys, or on a touch screen play them with several fingers and slide across them for
a glissando.

Left of it is the pitch wheel. Drag it up or down to bend the notes; it springs back when you let go. The **Up** and
**Down** boxes beside it set how far it bends, in semitones (0 to 24, 2 by default), and the preset keeps them.

## Step 3: pick a preset

<Figure name='presets' alt='The Presets dialog of Chisper: the Popular tab, the search field and a list of presets, each with the number of its listeners' narrow>

Click the name of the preset in the header to open the dialog. A click on a row plays that preset at once and the
dialog stays open, so you can go through them one after another.

</Figure>

- **Popular** lists the featured presets and the ones listeners stay with; the number beside a preset is how many
  listeners stayed with it.
- **All** lists every preset, sorted by **New**, **A–Z** or **Popular**. Typing in *Search all presets* searches
  them all by name.
- **◀ ▶** beside the name in the header step through the list you were in (the popular presets, or your last
  search), so you don't even need the dialog.
- The circular arrow, **Init preset**, loads *Init*: a plain sound of one oscillator with the rest switched off.
  Your own sounds start here.
- A **\*** after the name means you have changed the sound since it was opened or saved.
- Every preset has a link of its own (`chisper.qvantor.org/?preset=…`): the address bar shows it as soon as you
  pick one.

## What is on the screen?

<InterfaceMap parts={['logo', 'spectrum', 'screens', 'presets', 'master', 'sub', 'osc1', 'osc2', 'noise', 'filter', 'env', 'lfo', 'velo', 'voicing', 'wheel', 'keys', 'clip']} alt='The whole screen of Chisper on a computer with its parts numbered: the header; the sub, the oscillators, the noise and the filter in the top row; the envelopes, LFOs, velocity and voicing under them; the keys at the bottom'>

1. **Logo**: opens this site in a new tab. The synth keeps playing in its own.
2. **Spectrum**: the frequencies coming out of the synth right now, low on the left, high on the right.
3. **OSC | FX**: switches the top row between the sources of the sound and the effects (distortion, chorus,
   delay, compressor and reverb, in any order you drag them into).
4. **Presets**: the name, ◀ ▶ and the circular arrow of *Init*; under them **Save**, **Share**, **Midi** (your
   [MIDI keyboard](/manual/midi-keyboard-setup/)) and the gear (the latency of the sound, this manual).
5. **Master**: the volume of everything, −40 to +12 dB, with a meter beside it. A limiter after it keeps the peaks
   from clipping.
6. **Sub**: a sub oscillator for weight in the low end: a sine or one of five other simple shapes, up to four
   octaves away from the note.
7. **Osc 1**: the first [wavetable oscillator](/manual/wavetables/). The table and its arrows on top, the pitch
   (**Oct**, **Semi**, **Fine**), the wave that plays, and **Gain**, **Wave**, **Detune** and **Blend** under it.
8. **Osc 2**: the second one, the same in every way; *Init* has it off.
9. **Noise**: a noise oscillator in every voice: white, pink and brown noise, hats, vinyl crackle and more, looped
   or played once.
10. **Filter**: a filter in every voice, eight types, with **Cutoff**, **Res** and **Gain**.
11. **ENV 1–3**: [envelopes](/manual/modulation-envelopes-lfo/). ENV 1 is the volume of every note.
12. **LFO 1–4**: [LFOs](/manual/modulation-envelopes-lfo/) that move any knob you drag them onto.
13. **VELO**: what the velocity of a note does.
14. **Voicing**: **Poly** and how many notes play at once (1 to 12, 8 by default), **Legato** and
    **Portamento**.
15. **Pitch wheel** and the octave of the computer keyboard.
16. **Keys**: the on-screen piano.
17. **Clip**: the loop of the piano roll. A click opens the roll over the synth, ▶ plays the clip.

</InterfaceMap>

A part with a box in its title bar can be switched off and on: click the title. *Init* starts with Osc 1 on and
Osc 2, Noise, Sub and Filter off.

## First knobs: do it, hear it

A knob turns when you drag it: up or right raises it, down or left lowers it. Its value shows while you hold it.
Right-click a knob (on a touch screen, hold it) for its menu; **Default** there puts it back. A double-click does
nothing, so reach for the menu.

Load *Init* (the circular arrow by the name), hold a note with one hand and turn with the other.

### Wave

*Init* plays the table *Basic Shapes*: nine basic waves. Turn **Wave** on Osc 1 and the tone steps from one wave to
the next; the strip under the wave shows where you are (Wave 3/9). On a table of 256 frames the same turn is a
smooth sweep, and that sweep is what wavetable synthesis is about.

### Detune and Blend

Every oscillator plays a unison of voices on each note: *Init* has two (**Unison** under the wave, up to 8).
**Detune** (0 to 100, 5 by default) spreads them in pitch: turn it up and the note gets wider and more chorused.
**Blend** sets the level of the centre voices against the side ones, 75% by default.

### Cutoff

Switch the Filter on with a click on its title. It is a lowpass with its cutoff at 440 Hz by default (the knob
shows the hertz without the unit). Turn **Cutoff** down and the sound gets darker, up and it opens; raise **Res**
and a whistle rises at the cutoff.

## Exercise: your first sound from scratch

<Try title='From Init to a supersaw lead in five moves' preset='supersaw-lead'>

1. Load *Init* (the circular arrow by the name) and hold <kbd>Q</kbd>.
2. Set **Unison** on Osc 1 to 8 and turn **Detune** to about 30.
3. Turn **Wave** to the last wave of *Basic Shapes*.
4. Switch the Filter on and bring **Cutoff** down to about 1000.
5. Raise **Res** a little.

You should hear a single thin tone turn into a wide lead, then lose its brightest edge as the filter closes. The
preset below is that sound finished: 8 voices at Detune 30 on a saw table, an envelope opening the filter on every
note.

</Try>

## How do I save and share a sound without an account?

1. Press **Save** (or <kbd>⌘ S</kbd> / <kbd>Ctrl S</kbd>).
2. Give it a name of up to 40 characters. Names are unique, capitals or not: a taken one says so as you type.
3. Press **Save**. The dialog shows the link of your preset and **Copy link**.

Whoever opens the link hears your sound: every knob, every modulation and every effect. **Share** in the header
copies the link of the preset again later. It plays the sound as it was saved, so save again before you share a
change.

There is no account: the browser you saved from keeps a key to the preset, and that browser can overwrite it later
(**Save** then offers **Save** and **Save as new**). Another browser, another device or cleared site data can't
overwrite it, only save a new one.

If the piano roll has notes, the dialog also offers **Default clip**: the preset then opens with that melody, and
its link plays it too.

<Note>

A preset is removed 90 days after you last saved or opened it, unless by then at least two people have each played
it for a minute. From then on it stays.

</Note>

## Chisper on a phone

<Figure name='phone-keys' alt='Chisper on a phone in the Keys mode: the synth above two rows of keys, the octave keys and the pitch wheel' phone>

The Keys mode of a phone: the synth on top, two rows of keys under it.

</Figure>

Phones and tablets get a layout of their own (a large tablet on its side shows the full screen of a computer). Its
header has the same presets, **Save** and **Share**, the screens **OSC**, **FX** and **CLIP**, and two modes:

- **Keys** (the default): a small live view of the synth over two rows of keys, C4 over C3, one octave each held
  upright and two on its side. Each row has octave keys of its own; the pitch wheel is at the side.
- **Synth**: the parts of the synth one under the other (Sub, Osc 1, Osc 2, Noise, Filter, ENV, LFO, VELO,
  Voicing) to scroll through; **CLIP** opens the piano roll on the whole screen.

On an iPhone or iPad the sound plays even with the silent switch on.
