Plug a USB MIDI keyboard into a computer, a Chromebook or an Android phone, open Chisper in Chrome, Edge, Opera or
Firefox and allow MIDI when the browser asks: the keyboard plays at once, with velocity and pitch bend. Safari, and
every browser on iPhone and iPad, has no Web MIDI: play the keys on the screen or the computer keyboard there. A
DAW plays Chisper the same way through a virtual MIDI port, its MIDI Clock leading the tempo.

## Which browsers and devices work?

<CompatTable />

Every browser needs WebAssembly SIMD and AudioWorklet, the engine of the sound: Chrome and Edge 91 or later,
Firefox 89 or later, Safari 16.4 or later. **Latency choice** is the setting below: only Chromium on a computer
takes it.

## How do I connect a MIDI keyboard?

1. Plug the keyboard in, before or after you open Chisper: it shows up either way.
2. Open [chisper.qvantor.org](/). The browser asks whether the site may use your MIDI devices: allow it. Chrome
   and Edge ask in a small window by the address bar; Firefox asks you to add a small site permission add-on for
   MIDI.
3. Chisper picks the first keyboard it finds and says so (*… connected*). Play.

The sound starts with your first click, tap or key press on the page: if you have played only MIDI so far, click
the page once.

## The Midi menu

<Figure name='midi' alt='The MIDI menu of Chisper: the IN lamp, a keyboard selected under Input, the Channel keys All and 1 to 16, and the Sync to MIDI Clock lamp waiting for a clock' narrow>

**Midi** in the header: the input, its channel and the clock.

</Figure>

- **Input**: your devices. Click one to play from it; a dot on the **Midi** key shows one is chosen. A device
  plugged in later shows up here, and the one you chose comes back by itself when you plug it in again. The tab
  keeps your choice through a reload.
- **Channel**: **All** (the default) or one of 1 to 16. A channel key lights up while a note plays on it, so you see
  which channel your keyboard sends.
- **IN** lights up while you hold a key: the quickest check that notes arrive.
- **Sync to MIDI Clock**: see below.

Changing the input or the channel lets go of the notes held on the old one.

## What does Chisper read from MIDI?

- **Notes and velocity.** Velocity is the [VELO](/manual/modulation-envelopes-lfo/) source: by default it sets the
  volume of each note. The same note held on two channels is one voice.
- **Pitch bend**, as far as the **Up** and **Down** boxes beside the pitch wheel say (0 to 24 semitones, 2 by
  default; the preset keeps them).
- **MIDI Clock** from a DAW or a drum machine, with Start, Stop, Continue and Song Position. With **Sync to MIDI
  Clock** on (the default), the tempo of Chisper follows the clock after one beat of it: the lamp goes from
  *Waiting for a clock* to the tempo (*120 BPM*), and the tempo box of the piano roll shows **EXT**. Half a second
  without a clock and Chisper keeps its own tempo again. Stop stops the piano roll where it is; the LFOs keep
  running.

Not read yet: Control Change (the mod wheel, a sustain pedal, knobs and faders), aftertouch and program changes.
Chisper sends no MIDI out.

## How do I play Chisper from a DAW and sync it to the song?

Chisper takes MIDI from any program, not only from a keyboard: a DAW such as Ableton Live, Logic Pro, FL Studio,
Cubase, Reaper or Bitwig can play it from a MIDI track and lead its tempo with MIDI Clock. The DAW sends to a
virtual MIDI port, and the browser sees that port as one more MIDI device.

1. **Make a virtual MIDI port.** On a Mac, open *Audio MIDI Setup*, choose *Window → Show MIDI Studio*,
   double-click *IAC Driver* and check *Device is online*. On Windows, install a free virtual port driver such as
   loopMIDI and add a port.
2. **Send the DAW to it.** Set the MIDI output of a track to that port and send MIDI Clock to the same port. The
   names differ from DAW to DAW: in Ableton Live, *Settings → Link, Tempo & MIDI* and **Sync** on the output of the
   port; in Logic Pro, *File → Project Settings → Synchronization → MIDI* and *Transmit MIDI Clock*; in FL Studio,
   *Options → MIDI settings*, the output port with *Send master sync*.
3. **Pick it in Chisper.** Open **Midi**, choose the port (*IAC Driver Bus 1*, *loopMIDI Port*) as the **Input**
   and keep **Sync to MIDI Clock** on. Click the page once: MIDI alone doesn't start the sound.
4. **Press play in the DAW.** The lamp shows its tempo and the tempo box of the piano roll shows **EXT**. Every
   LFO, envelope time and delay set in BPM follows the song, and the notes of the track play Chisper.

- Start, Stop, Continue and Song Position come along. Press Play in the piano roll of Chisper while the clock
  leads: the clip waits for the Start of the DAW, then plays in time with the song, from where the DAW starts.
- With the anchor key of an LFO on, a Free LFO stays on the beats of the song.
- Chisper listens to one input. To play your keyboard too, let the DAW take it and pass it on through the same port
  (on Windows that is the way anyway: a device is often open in one program only).
- The sound comes out of the browser a little after the DAW sends a note: the browser takes MIDI in between its
  other work and adds the latency of its audio (Low keeps it short). Most DAWs can send a track a little early to
  make up for it. Keep Chisper in a window of its own beside the DAW, not in a hidden tab: browsers slow hidden
  tabs down.
- Chisper sends no audio to the DAW. To record it there, route the output of the browser into the DAW with a
  loopback audio driver (BlackHole on a Mac, VB-CABLE on Windows), or record the output of your audio interface.

## Playing without a MIDI keyboard

The letters of the computer keyboard are two rows of a piano, <kbd>Z</kbd> = C3 and <kbd>Q</kbd> = C4, and the
arrows <kbd>↓</kbd> <kbd>↑</kbd> move them by an octave (0 to 6). They play by the place of a key, not its letter,
so the same keys play on QWERTY, QWERTZ and AZERTY. Every note has velocity 100.

<KeyboardMap />

On a touch screen, the keys at the bottom of the screen play with several fingers at once, and sliding across them
plays a glissando. More in [Getting started](/manual/getting-started/).

## Does Chisper work on a Chromebook?

Yes. Chrome on a Chromebook is Chrome on a computer: MIDI keyboards over USB, the latency choice, the computer
keyboard and the on-screen piano all work. There is nothing to install, so Chisper runs on school and work
Chromebooks where you can't add software, and it doesn't need the Linux apps of ChromeOS.

## Does Chisper work on an iPhone or iPad?

Yes, in Safari on iOS and iPadOS 16.4 or later (every browser of an iPhone or iPad uses the engine of Safari, so
Chrome there is the same). The sound plays even with the silent switch on.

There is no Web MIDI on iOS, so a MIDI keyboard can't play Chisper there: use the two rows of keys of the phone
layout (the **Keys** mode), or the on-screen piano on an iPad. Drawing a wave in the
[wavetable editor](/manual/wavetables/) works with a finger or an Apple Pencil.

On Android, Chrome has Web MIDI: a USB MIDI keyboard through a USB-C adapter plays as on a computer.

## Latency: Auto, Low and Normal

<Figure name='settings' alt='The Settings menu of Chisper: Latency with Auto (on Low), Low and Normal, the note Switches to Normal if the sound crackles, and the count of dropouts since the page opened' narrow>

The gear in the header: the latency and how many dropouts the sound has had since the page opened. Under them is
the link to this manual.

</Figure>

Latency is the time between pressing a key and hearing it. **Low** answers faster; **Normal** gives the computer
more room, so the sound stays steady on a slow one. In Chrome on a Mac the browser plays 128 samples at a time at
Low and 256 at Normal.

- **Auto** (the default) starts at Low where the browser can count dropouts (recent Chrome and Edge), else at
  Normal, and shows what it picked under its name.
- If the sound drops out in three different seconds within a minute at Low, Chisper asks *The sound crackles.
  Switch to Normal for a steady sound?*, and Auto keeps Normal from then on. The note under the keys then
  says *Low crackled on this computer*; its link **Try low again** makes Auto start at Low again.
- A change reloads the page; the tab keeps the sound and the MIDI input.
- Only Chromium on a computer (Chrome, Edge, Opera, Chrome on a Chromebook) lets a page choose. Safari, Firefox
  and phones set the latency themselves, and the menu says *This browser sets the latency itself*.

## Troubleshooting

### No sound

- **Click the page, tap it or press a key** once: browsers start the sound only then, and a MIDI note doesn't
  count.
- Check the **Master** knob (all the way down is −∞) and the volume of the tab, of the system and of the output
  you listen to.
- *Oscillators can't play: Not supported by this browser*: the browser lacks one of AudioWorklet, Web Workers,
  MessageChannel or WebAssembly SIMD. Update it (Safari 16.4 or later) or use Chrome, Edge or Firefox.
- *Oscillators can't play: Needs HTTPS or localhost*: open [https://chisper.qvantor.org](/).
- *Failed to load* under the oscillators, or *No sound: the master limiter failed*: press **Retry**. A preset
  being opened is silent for a moment too, until its tables arrive.

### Crackles and dropouts

- Choose **Normal** in the latency (Chrome and Edge on a computer).
- Fewer voices cost less: lower **Unison** or the number of **Poly** voices.
- Close other heavy tabs and apps, and plug a laptop in: power saving can slow the sound engine down.

### My MIDI keyboard doesn't show up

- The Midi menu says *MIDI isn't available: no Web MIDI in this browser, or access was denied.* Either the browser
  has no Web MIDI (Safari, any browser on iOS) or the MIDI permission was refused: allow MIDI devices for
  chisper.qvantor.org in the site settings of the browser and reload.
- It says *No MIDI device connected. Plug one in, it shows up here.* Try another cable (some USB cables only
  charge) and another port, and check that the keyboard is on and in its USB MIDI mode.
- On Windows, a MIDI device can often be open in only one program at a time: close your DAW or any other music
  app that uses the keyboard, then reload the page.
- Notes arrive (the **IN** lamp lights) but nothing plays: set **Channel** to **All**, or to the channel whose key
  lights up.
